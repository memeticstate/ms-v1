Getting started

Website tour

The website is organized around orientation, investigation, and memory, with PONS launches as its main indexed subject. This tour explains the role of each section. Available data, filters, and inspection depth depend on the current release and connected sources.

The screenshots show the interface using a public snapshot from 9 September 2026. Their counts and relative times are static examples; use the website's own observation times for a new investigation. Select an image to open it at full size.

Atlas

Atlas is the map. It puts objects and relationships beside each other so you can explore a neighborhood instead of treating every token as an isolated chart.

Look for recurring connections, new relationships, and clusters whose shape is changing. An edge indicates an observed relationship within the displayed coverage; it does not establish that two projects are affiliated.

Signals

Signals focus on what changed. A useful signal identifies an observation, its time window, and why it may deserve investigation.

Before relying on a short description, check the available evidence and freshness information. “Investigate next” points to a question to explore. It is not a trade instruction.

Lifecycle

Lifecycle organizes objects by their development over time: early formation, recurring participation, stress, fading, and venue-specific transitions where applicable.

A state label summarizes a period of observation. Compare the underlying activity and liquidity with the earlier window before interpreting a transition as meaningful.

Quote-asset ecology

The Quote-asset ecology table appears within Signals and Atlas. It compares PONS launches grouped by their quote asset, including recorded activity, participant addresses, lifecycle maturity, and creator concentration. Select a quote-asset group to narrow your investigation.

These are PONS quote-asset groups, not a PAIR factory index. Shared quote assets can explain apparent similarities without implying shared ownership or coordinated communities.

An unresolved quote identity can appear as QUOTE- followed by a short contract prefix. That is a placeholder for a quote asset, not another indexed venue.

Indexed history

History provides the earlier observations behind the current view. Its depth depends on what was collected and successfully backfilled.

An absent event can reflect a collection gap. It is not sufficient evidence that nothing happened. Use the recorded coverage and source links when they are available.

Field Guide and premium

The Field Guide explains the vocabulary behind these sections. Return to it whenever a label is unclear.

Premium Field Operator adds a protected PONS V2 research workspace. Open the Premium tab or choose Open full research from a token dossier. Connect a holder wallet, verify access, and select an indexed token or paste its contract.

A useful first session

  1. Orient yourself in Atlas.
  2. Pick one change from Signals.
  3. Check the object's identity, source, and observation time.
  4. Compare its available lifecycle and quote-asset context.
  5. Write down what would strengthen or weaken your interpretation.

See coverage and freshness before treating a current-looking card as current data.
