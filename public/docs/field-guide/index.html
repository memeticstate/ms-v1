Getting started

The Field Guide

Energy flows to what is alive

Markets produce attention, imitation, affiliation, and memory as well as trades. On a public chain, some of those relationships leave observable traces: launches, pools, transfers, recurring participation, and changes in liquidity.

A transaction tells us that something happened. Understanding whether it matters takes context.

Memetic State starts with four questions:

  1. What is alive? Where is participation recurring and becoming more organized?
  2. How do we know? Which observations support that reading?
  3. What should we investigate next? Which uncertainty matters most?
  4. What deserves to be remembered? Which changes remain meaningful after the initial burst?

“Alive” is a working interpretation of behavior over time. It does not mean safe, valuable, endorsed, or likely to rise in price.

Reading the terrain

The original field-guide metaphor is simple: stocks are the terrain, memecoins are the species, and pair pools are the links between them.

A pairing can connect a cultural object with a recognizable financial reference. Repeated pairings, changes in liquidity, and recurring participants can make a relationship worth studying. The metaphor helps you navigate; it does not establish collateral, equity ownership, endorsement, or a causal relationship between prices.

The current observatory is centered on PONS launches on Robinhood Chain. Atlas groups those launches by their quote asset. A quote pairing is a market relationship, not a reference to the separate PAIR protocol. See coverage and freshness for the precise scope of each connected source.

The language of state

Label What it asks you to inspect
Forming Is early activity developing into repeated participation?
Alive Does the evidence show persistence, breadth, or recurring attention?
Coherent Is there a relationship or cluster that can be described and tested?
Stressed Is liquidity, participation, or holder structure deteriorating?
Fading Is the evidence of continued participation weakening?
Graduated Has a launch crossed a venue-specific condition? The meaning depends on the venue.
Remembered Is this transition useful enough to preserve as a case study?

These labels summarize a view of the evidence. They can change as new information arrives. Some venues have no graduation process at all.

From map to investigation

Start with orientation. Find a cluster, launch, or relationship that catches your attention. Then inspect the underlying object: its identity, available history, source, and observation window.

Consider two launches. One has a brief burst of trades concentrated in a few wallets. The other has smaller activity that continues across several periods. Both can be interesting, but they support different conclusions. Transaction count alone cannot tell you which has developed durable participation; multiple wallets do not necessarily mean independent people.

A useful investigation names an alternative explanation. Did the pattern come from recurring community interest, a venue's default settings, a single actor, or incomplete data? What observation would help distinguish those possibilities?

Building memory

Memory gives an observation a second life. A preserved case can show how a relationship formed, what the evidence looked like at the time, and what happened later.

Failed or fading launches can be useful cases too. The aim is to learn from the whole lifecycle, rather than preserve only outcomes that flatter an earlier interpretation.
