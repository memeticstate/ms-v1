Access and token

Creator fees and buybacks

Status: proposed operating program. The allocation, effective date, execution setup, and first report have not been announced.

The funding source

The recurring funding source contemplated here is creator fees actually received by the project under its launch platform's rules. Trading volume, a claimable balance, token market capitalization, and unrealized appreciation are not spendable receipts.

Each claim needs to be recorded in its actual asset. A balance in NVDA is not a USD balance, and amounts in different assets cannot be added without an explicit valuation method.

Founder token-sale proceeds are separate. A founder contribution can be recorded if one is made, but it should not be presented as recurring creator-fee revenue.

Intended use of future claims

The plan is to direct a portion of received creator fees toward open-market token buybacks while retaining enough flexibility to fund the website, data, development, and .

There is no permanent percentage pledge in this version. The project will publish the active policy and effective date before describing the program as operational. Individual budget reports will show amounts for operations, , buybacks, and retained funds. Any developer compensation paid from the treasury will be identified as an operating expense.

Claims will be reported when made. Buybacks may be accumulated into practical execution batches or deferred when balances are small, costs are disproportionate, or execution conditions are unsuitable. A budget allocation is not a completed purchase; deferred amounts remain visible in the records.

Changes to the program will be dated and explained. A changed policy does not rewrite earlier receipts or turn previously allocated funds into an undisclosed expense.

What counts as a buyback or burn?

Action What can be claimed
Fees arrive Creator-fee revenue was received.
Funds are budgeted An amount is allocated; it has not necessarily been spent.
Tokens are bought on the open market A buyback occurred, with an execution transaction and received quantity.
Bought tokens remain in the treasury Treasury-held tokens; they may still be transferable.
Tokens are locked A lock, with its actual terms and duration.
Tokens are verifiably burned A burn, with the disposition transaction and method disclosed.

The token's supported method determines whether a burn reduces reported total supply or instead makes tokens inaccessible. Those outcomes will be described accurately. A purchase alone, or a temporary lock, will not be called a burn.

What is the Pons inspiration?

Pons's published v1 documentation describes allocating 80% of protocol fees to buybacks and burns and 20% to infrastructure and the team, with manual initiation through an automated TWAP process. That is a reference for transparent reinvestment, not Memetic State's adopted split or evidence of this token's fee settings. Protocol revenue and a token creator's receipts are different funding bases. Pons documentation

What the public record will show

For each reporting period: opening balances; received claims and transaction links; actual expenses; buyback amounts and transactions; acquired-token disposition; and closing balances. The record will identify any unresolved reconciliation rather than silently count it as available money.

Completed buybacks will be reported after execution. They are not a promise to defend a market price. See the historical claim disclosure for the earlier banner-related claim.
